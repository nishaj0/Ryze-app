# Ryze end-user guide

Ryze is a workout-planning and training-log app. It helps you set up a practical split, log sets, view exercise-specific progress, track measurements and photos, and use AI-assisted coaching with your approval before any changes are made.

## 1. Set up your training profile

On first use, complete onboarding with your goal, experience, available days, equipment, body details, sleep, and preferred split. Ryze uses this information to recommend a split that fits your schedule.

You can choose a prebuilt split or make a custom split. The built-in options include Full Body (3 days), Push/Pull/Legs, and Bro Split. The AI Split Builder can also produce a plan around the limits you provide.

## 2. Complete a workout

1. Open the Home screen and select the scheduled workout.
2. Review the exercise list, target sets, rep range, rest guidance, and notes.
3. Record each completed set with its weight and repetitions.
4. Use the exercise screen to review technique notes, equipment, and alternatives when available.
5. Finish the session to save it to your workout history and review the workout summary.

Each completed workout keeps a historical snapshot of its split day. If you later change your split, earlier sessions still show the day name they were performed under.

If you intentionally do not train, use the rest-day flow and select the reason that best matches the day. Your workout history keeps training and declared rest days together so your schedule remains understandable.

## 3. Use progressive-overload guidance

The Progress area includes an exercise-specific Progressive Overload section. Choose an exercise and optionally narrow it by muscle group or split day.

For each exercise, Ryze shows:

- Your latest and previous top set.
- Current weight, reps, estimated one-rep maximum, and training volume.
- A recommended next step, such as keeping the load, adding reps, or increasing weight.
- Logged-session history so you can judge the recommendation in context.

The recommendation is based on your real, logged sets. It is guidance, not a command: adjust or skip it when form, recovery, pain, or your coach’s instructions call for a different choice.

## 4. Track your progress

Use Progress to review your training over time:

| Area | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Dashboard | Recent training trends, workout activity, and high-level progress |
| Exercise Progress | Exercise history and exercise-specific overload guidance |
| Records | Personal records from completed sessions |
| Metrics | Bodyweight and other measurements you record |
| Training heatmap | Completed-workout activity by date |
| Muscle volume | Volume grouped by muscle group |
| Photos | Capture, upload, timeline, comparison, and deletion of progress photos |

Progress calculations only use completed, logged data. Missing or incomplete sets do not become training records.

### Capture and compare photos

Use Photos to take or choose a progress photo, upload it, and add it to your private timeline. The comparison view helps you place two photos side by side. You can delete photos you no longer want to keep. Uploads are processed before they appear in the timeline, so wait for the upload status to finish before leaving the screen.

## 5. Ryze Coach and check-ins

Ryze Coach can answer questions about your active plan and recent training. It can read relevant workout, split, check-in, and progress information to provide context-aware help.

After a workout or when prompted, submit a check-in. Describe how the session felt and any exercise that was difficult. Ryze can summarize the sentiment and identify recurring trouble areas.

The Coach may prepare an action such as opening a relevant screen, suggesting a rest day, or proposing an exercise swap. Any action that changes your plan requires your confirmation first.

## 6. Suggestions, reminders, and support

Repeated difficult check-ins can result in a plan suggestion, for example reducing volume, changing rest, using a deload, or swapping an exercise. Review the explanation and accept or dismiss the suggestion; nothing is applied automatically.

You can manage reminders and app preferences in Settings. Use Support to request help or report a problem, and use Request Exercise when the exercise catalogue does not include what you need.

## Safety and practical limits

AI responses and calculated recommendations are training aids, not medical diagnosis or treatment. Stop and seek an appropriate qualified professional for pain, injury, illness, or health concerns. Make load changes only when your technique and recovery support them.

## Quick troubleshooting

- **A workout is not on the right day:** review the active split in Profile and switch or edit the split if needed.
- **No overload data appears:** complete and save at least one workout containing logged weight and rep sets for that exercise.
- **A recommendation feels wrong:** do not follow it automatically; use your judgement and adjust your next set manually.
- **A plan change was suggested:** open it, review the reason and alternatives, then accept or dismiss it.
- **An exercise is missing:** submit an exercise request from the Exercises section.
